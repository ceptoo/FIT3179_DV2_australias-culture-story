Australia's Culture Story

How the languages we speak, the places we protect, the people we are and the culture we fund have changed.

By Nigel · FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · October 2026

1. The first cultures

Long before 1788, this continent held hundreds of languages. Records of 834 of them still carry a location.

In 2021, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people used 167 Indigenous languages at home. But only 139 were being used by children, which is how a language survives.

2. The places we protect

Since 2004, the National Heritage List has recognised 126 places of outstanding value to the nation: Indigenous, natural and historic.

3. Who we became

In 1901 most migrants came from Britain and Ireland. Today, people born in India, China and the Philippines are reshaping who Australians are.

4. How we take part

The 2021–22 survey ran during COVID-19 restrictions, so it captures a culture on pause.

Every state lost audiences, but Victoria, with the longest lockdowns, lost the most.

By 2025, 74% of Australians had attended a live arts event or festival in the past year, the highest since the national survey began in 2009.

5. Libraries and the stage

Public libraries lent 174 million items in 2023–24, the most in five years, and almost a third of them were digital.

Each Australian now borrows more, but after inflation, spending on libraries per person has fallen by 12% since 2019–20.

Live performance collapsed in 2020, then came back bigger than before: a record 31.4 million tickets in 2024, almost half of them for contemporary music.
